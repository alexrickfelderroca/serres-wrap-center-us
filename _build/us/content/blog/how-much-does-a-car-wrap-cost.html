A wrap that stops at the panel edge instead of folding into the door shuts costs less to do, and in a climate with daily heat, afternoon downpours and salt air off the coast, those exposed edges are exactly where the film starts to lift first.

Full Color Change: what the price includes (and what it doesn't)

A well-executed full wrap is not a matter of laying film over the bodywork. A Full Color Change takes from three to four days in our studio, a Signature Wrap from five to seven, and the price has to cover every one of these steps:

  • Complete paint decontamination. Love-bug residue, tree sap, bird droppings and the hard-water spots left by lawn sprinklers all have to come off before the film goes on. Any trace of wax or sap underneath lifts a wrap within months.
  • Trim removal. Door handles, lights, moldings and badges. Wrapping around a part instead of underneath it is visible from a few feet away, and it leaves an edge for heat and rain to work on.
  • Wrapped edges. The film has to fold into the door, hood and tailgate shuts. That is what separates a color change from a commercial graphic, and it is what keeps humidity out from under the film.
  • Post-installation inspection. In our case, a documented panel-by-panel check before the car is handed back.

When a full-wrap quote comes in far below the pack price, one of these steps is missing. It is usually the disassembly, the wrapped edges or the quality of the film itself. Always ask for the breakdown in writing.

Car wrap cost by film grade: 3M, Avery Dennison and Inozetek

The film is one of the biggest lines in the final price of a full wrap, and not every vinyl is fit to wrap a car:

  • Cast film. The standard for a color change: 3M 2080 series, Avery Dennison Supreme Wrapping Film or Inozetek Super Gloss. It conforms to complex curves without shrinking back, and it carries the maker's outdoor durability rating. In South Florida that rating matters more than anywhere: year-round UV and hood temperatures that climb well past the air temperature shorten the life of any film, and the exact figure depends on the film maker's rating for this climate zone.
  • Calendered film. Made for commercial signage on flat surfaces. It costs a fraction of cast film, but on a curved hood it shrinks, shows seams and fades quickly under Florida sun. If a full-wrap quote is suspiciously low, this is almost always why.
  • Special finishes. Colour-flip, satin metallics, brushed and forged-carbon textures cost more per roll and take more hours to lay. That is why the Signature Wrap level exists.

In our car wrap service we work only with cast film from 3M (2080 series), Avery Dennison and Inozetek, with more than 150 colors in a physical swatch catalog so you can see them on a real curve in daylight rather than on a screen.

Partial wrap: what Accents cost

You do not need to wrap the whole car to change its presence. A partial wrap is the most cost-effective way in, and the Accents pack starts at {{PRICE:wrap.accents}}. The pieces we wrap most often:

  • Roof. The classic gloss-black or satin roof, and also the panel that takes the most sun in Florida, so the film choice matters.
  • Hood. The second most-requested panel, and the one that meets love bugs and highway grit head-on. If protection matters more than color here, PPF may be the better tool.
  • Mirror caps. Small, quick and effective in carbon-look or gloss black.
  • Pillars and window frames. The chrome delete that gives a car a cleaner, more modern outline.
  • Diffuser, splitter and trim. Small pieces that sharpen the lower body.

A well-chosen accents package, say roof, mirrors and pillars in gloss black, transforms a factory finish from one day in the studio. It is also the sensible way to see how a shop works before trusting it with a full wrap.

Wrapping the whole car: cost versus a repaint

The comparison almost nobody puts on the table:

  • Quality full repaint: irreversible, keeps the car in the body shop for weeks of prep, paint and cure time, and the car loses its original-paint status at any future appraisal.
  • Full Color Change wrap: from {{PRICE:wrap.full}}, reversible in a day of work, and the factory paint sits protected underneath the film, shielded from the UV that fades clear coat, from love-bug acid and from sap and sprinkler spotting.

There are two more measurable advantages. The first is lead time: a Full Color Change is delivered in three to four days in the studio, while a proper repaint rarely comes back that quickly. The second is resale: you remove the wrap and hand over the car with its factory paint intact, something a demanding buyer notices immediately. In Miami, where the sun starts working on an unprotected clear coat from day one, a wrapped car has spent those years with its paint out of the sun.

If your priority is not a new color but protecting the paint from stone chips and road debris, the right tool is PPF: a self-healing polyurethane film, from {{PRICE:ppf.front}} for the Front Pack, with a 3-year film warranty. Its self-healing topcoat relaxes wash swirls and light scuffs out with the heat of the sun, which in Miami is most of the year. They are different products for different goals, and it is worth deciding which one you need before asking for a quote.

How to judge a car wrap quote

Five questions that separate a serious quote from a surprise six months later:

  1. Which brand and exact film reference? "High-quality vinyl" is not an answer. 3M 2080-G12 is.
  2. Are parts removed, or is the film wrapped around them? Ask for it in writing.
  3. Are the edges wrapped? Open a door on a finished car and look for yourself.
  4. What written warranty covers the installation and the film? And how does the film maker rate that film for a hot, high-UV zone like South Florida?
  5. Is the figure fixed, or "starting from"? Our packs start at a published price and every car gets a fixed quote after an in-person inspection.

Frequently asked questions about car wrap cost

How long does a car wrap last in Florida?

A cast film from 3M, Avery Dennison or Inozetek is rated by its maker for outdoor use, and that rating is shorter in a hot, high-UV zone like South Florida than in a temperate climate; the exact figure depends on the film and on the maker's rating for this zone. Cheap calendered film shrinks and fades much sooner. Parking under cover and washing by hand extend the life of any film.

Does wrapping a car damage the paint?

No, as long as the paint is factory or a properly cured respray. The adhesive is designed to come off without residue, and the film shields the clear coat from light scratches and UV. On poor-quality resprays there is a real risk of the paint lifting with the film, and a serious shop spots that before starting.

Do I need to tell anyone if I wrap my car a different color?

Tell your insurer about the color change so your policy reflects how the car actually looks, and check your registration details with the FLHSMV to see whether anything needs updating. It takes a few minutes and avoids confusion at a claim or a traffic stop.

How long does it take to wrap a whole car?

From three to four days in the studio for a Full Color Change and from five to seven for a Signature Wrap, depending on disassembly and body complexity. A roof or an accents package is done from one day. Be wary of anyone promising a full wrap in a day: rushed work shows up at the edges and in bubbles, and Miami heat finds every one of them.

Can I take a wrapped car through an automatic car wash?

Better not: brushes and rollers punish the edges of the film. Wash by hand with a pH-neutral shampoo, or with a pressure washer kept at least a foot from the panel. In Florida, rinse off love-bug residue, bird droppings and sap as soon as you can, because heat bakes them into any finish, and dry the car after sprinkler contact to avoid hard-water spots.
