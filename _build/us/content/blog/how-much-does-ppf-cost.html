What PPF is and why its price varies so much

PPF is a clear thermoplastic polyurethane (TPU) film applied over the paint. It absorbs stone chips, parking scuffs and wash scratches, and it takes the chemical hits that Florida throws at a car all year: love-bug residue in May and September, bird droppings, tree sap and the mineral spots left behind by sprinklers and hard water. The high-end films we install are self-healing: micro-scratches disappear with heat, and a hood parked in the Miami sun supplies that heat every afternoon.

The price varies so much because there is no single "PPF". Four variables change: the surface covered, the brand and grade of film, the size of the car and the hours of preparation. A partial front end on a compact and a full body on a three-row SUV are different jobs, with different materials and different studio times. That is why two quotes for "PPF" can be so far apart.

Front Pack vs Pro: the zone that takes the hits first

If the budget is limited, the front end is the investment with the best return. Bumper, hood and front fenders take the vast majority of the stone chips on the highway, and in South Florida they also take the sandblasting of wind-blown grit after every storm and the acidic residue of two love-bug seasons a year. A hood with a few years of I-95 and Turnpike miles shows it at a glance.

There are two levels:

  • Front Pack (from {{PRICE:ppf.front}}). Covers the bumper, the leading section of the hood and the mirrors. It protects where gravel hits hardest, at the cost of a visible edge line across the hood.
  • Pro (from {{PRICE:ppf.pro}}). Full hood and fenders, bumper, headlights and mirrors, with every edge wrapped around the panel and a Ceramic Coating applied over the film. No cut lines mid-panel: the finish is invisible, and it is the level we recommend on new cars.

The step up to Pro usually pays for itself. Repainting a chipped hood at a body shop means that panel no longer carries factory paint, and any appraiser or informed buyer picks that up at resale.

Full Body PPF: when it makes sense

Full Body protects every painted panel: sides, doors, rocker panels, roof and trunk lid. It is the pack for cars that live outdoors in Florida, where the horizontal panels take the full UV load, salt air reaches anything parked near the coast, and a sun-faded clear coat on the roof or hood is the first thing that dates a car. Our Full Body pack starts at {{PRICE:ppf.full}} with self-healing film, edges tucked and a Ceramic Coating over the film, and the whole job is done in-house in our own studio.

It makes sense in three scenarios:

  1. A new car you plan to keep five years or more. The film ages instead of the paint, and it comes off leaving the factory clear coat intact.
  2. Special colors or delicate finishes. Matte paint, custom colors or limited editions where a partial repaint never matches the tone.
  3. Vehicles with high cosmetic depreciation. Sports and premium cars where "100% original paint" is worth real money at sale time.

The full job requires removing handles, trim and lights to wrap the film around every edge. That is 5 to 8 days in the studio. If a Full Body quote promises 24 hours, the edges will be cut on the panel rather than tucked, and it will show.

Paint protection film: price by material and preparation

Behind two apparently identical quotes there can be very different jobs. These are the factors that move the price of paint protection film:

  • Brand and grade of film. Films from professional brands with a self-healing topcoat cost more than generic films without one. In Florida the difference shows early: cheap films yellow and lose clarity under year-round UV long before a quality film does.
  • A real warranty. Professional-brand films come with a written manufacturer warranty against yellowing, cracking and delamination; in our case, a 3-year film warranty. A film with no declared brand rarely offers any.
  • Surface preparation. The film seals whatever is underneath. A decontamination and polish beforehand (in our case with a documented panel-by-panel inspection under controlled lighting before delivery) adds hours, but it stops swirls, holograms and hard-water etching from being locked in for the life of the film.
  • Disassembly and wrapped edges. Wrapping edges means removing parts. It costs more than trimming the film on the body, and it is what separates an invisible finish from a merely correct one.
  • Size and complexity of the car. A Porsche 911 and a Ford Explorer need different amounts of film and present different curves and difficulty.

Is PPF worth it in Florida? Run the numbers

It depends on the car and on how long you plan to keep it. The honest calculation:

  • Annualized cost. Spread the price of a Pro or Full Body pack over the years you expect to own the car, and set that figure against a single body-shop repaint of the hood and bumper, plus the loss of factory paint that comes with it.
  • The cost of not protecting it. Repainting hood and bumper is a body-shop bill. A side panel scraped in a parking garage is another. And every repaint subtracts value: an informed buyer pays less for a car with repainted panels.
  • Resale value. Original paint with factory thickness readings is a measurable selling point. In premium segments, the difference between "original" and "partial repaint" can exceed the cost of the film itself.
  • The Florida factor. Unprotected paint here works harder than the same paint in a milder climate: the UV index and panel temperatures stay high most of the year, humidity and salt air go to work on any chip, and love-bug residue etches the clear coat if it bakes on for days. Protection that is optional up north is closer to maintenance in Miami.

When does it not pay off? On a car whose paint is already deteriorated (the film does not fix deep damage, it preserves it), on a short two-year lease where you never capitalize on the resale value, or if the budget only stretches to a generic film with no warranty. In that case, a Ceramic Coating with paint correction beforehand (from {{PRICE:ceramic.essential}} in our case, with a rated durability that depends on the pack) protects less against impacts, but it keeps the gloss and makes washing easier for a smaller investment. In this climate the hydrophobic layer earns its keep: afternoon thunderstorms, sprinkler water and humidity bead up and sheet off instead of drying into mineral spots.

How to compare PPF quotes without surprises

Before you sign, ask for these seven points in writing:

  1. The exact brand and model of the film, not just "quality PPF".
  2. The film's spec sheet: self-healing topcoat and resistance to yellowing, ideally with the maker's rating for hot, high-UV climates.
  3. The final, all-in price, with prep, disassembly and any ceramic top layer included.
  4. The list of panels covered and whether parts are removed to wrap the edges.
  5. Manufacturer and installer warranty, with years and specific coverage.
  6. Who installs the film and in which studio the work is done.
  7. What preparation is included: decontamination, polish and a documented final inspection.

A serious studio answers all seven without hedging. In our case, we publish the PPF service with packs, film and warranty in plain view, and every car leaves after a documented panel-by-panel inspection.

Frequently asked questions about PPF pricing

How much does front-end PPF cost?

At SERRES Miami the Front Pack starts at {{PRICE:ppf.front}} and the Pro pack, which covers the full front end with wrapped edges and a Ceramic Coating over the film, starts at {{PRICE:ppf.pro}}. Both use self-healing film from professional brands with a 3-year film warranty; the final figure depends on the model and the condition of the paint.

How long does PPF last in Florida?

A quality film, correctly installed, keeps its properties for years of use. The film we install is covered by a 3-year manufacturer warranty against yellowing, cracking and delamination. Florida's year-round UV and heat are harder on any film than a milder climate, so its life beyond that depends on the film maker's rating for this climate and on how the car is washed and parked. Generic, unbranded films are usually the first to yellow.

Does PPF damage the paint when it is removed?

No, as long as the film is a quality product and a professional removes it with controlled heat. The paint is left exactly as it was on installation day, which is the whole point. The risk exists with cheap films that have aged badly in the sun, or on repainted panels with poor adhesion.

Which is better, PPF or a ceramic coating?

They do different jobs. Ceramic Coating SiO₂ (from {{PRICE:ceramic.essential}} to {{PRICE:ceramic.concours}} in our case, paint correction included) adds gloss, hydrophobic behavior and easier washing, which matters against Florida rain, sprinklers and humidity, but it does not stop a stone chip. PPF physically protects the paint. The usual combination on premium cars: PPF on the front end and Ceramic Coating on the rest, or applied over the film itself.

Can PPF be installed on a used car?

Yes, as long as the paint is in good condition or is corrected first. The usual step is a polish to remove light scratches and water-spot etching, because the film preserves the surface exactly as it is underneath. On paint with deep damage, a sun-faded clear coat or low-quality repaints, it is worth assessing panel by panel.
