How much car upholstery cleaning costs in 2026

The price depends on three variables: the surface to be treated, the cleaning method and the condition of the interior. Vacuuming and refreshing fabric seats is not the same job as extracting coffee that has been baking into the foam for two summers, or reviving leather that has started to crack under the sun.

Florida adds a fourth: heat. A cabin parked in the open in July reaches dashboard temperatures that set spills into the fiber and dry leather out fast. The longer a stain has been cooking, the more passes it needs, and the more it costs.

What a professional upholstery cleaning includes

A serious service follows an ordered process. If a shop cannot walk you through these steps, be wary of the price, whether it is high or low.

  1. Deep vacuuming. Seats, rails, carpets, floor mats and trunk. Solid dirt, beach sand included, comes out before any product goes in; left in the fiber, sand works like sandpaper under every wet pass.
  2. Stain pre-treatment. Each stain is identified and treated with the right chemistry: tannins (coffee, wine, soda), oils and sunscreen, dyes or organic residue.
  3. Steam cleaning. Steam well above 212 °F dissolves dirt and disinfects without soaking the foam. It is the reference method for fabric and Alcantara, and it kills the mildew a damp Florida carpet breeds.
  4. Hot-water extraction. The machine injects cleaning solution and vacuums it straight back out together with the dissolved dirt. This is what separates a real cleaning from a brushing.
  5. Leather-specific treatment. pH-neutral cleaning followed by conditioning. Unfed leather dries out and cracks, our sun speeds that up, and a crack has no cheap fix.
  6. Controlled drying. A poorly dried interior smells of damp within a day or two, and mildew follows. Forced drying with filtered air prevents it; in a climate this humid it is the one step that cannot be skipped.

In a Deep Clean, that process means booking the car in for the day, which is why no express service can include all of it.

Interior cleaning cost by service level

The upholstery is the most visible part, but the interior is a whole. These are the three levels that make sense for most drivers:

  • Maintenance (Refresh). Every four to six weeks. Vacuum, plastics, glass and a once-over of the seats. It keeps the car presentable and stops sand, sunscreen and the haze a hot dashboard leaves on the inside of the windshield from setting in.
  • Deep cleaning (Deep Clean). Once or twice a year, ideally after the wet season. Steam and hot-water extraction across the whole upholstery, headliner included, plus leather cleaned and conditioned. It is the service most people mean when they ask for "upholstery cleaning".
  • Full detail (Showroom Reset). Before selling the car, after buying it used, or when the interior has gone years without a serious treatment. Adds the engine bay, trim and plastics restoration and a longer-lasting sealant on the paint outside.

A useful fact for deciding: a tired interior is one of the first things a buyer or a trade-in appraiser marks down, and a sun-faded, sticky cabin says neglect louder than a stone chip. A Showroom Reset is usually recovered in the sale price, and a Deep Clean almost always.

How to remove stains from car seats

Stains are the most common question. The general rule: the sooner it is treated, the cheaper it is. A fresh stain almost always comes out; one that has been set by heat and time may need several passes or remain as a permanent shadow. In a cabin that sits in the Florida sun, "set by heat" happens in days, not months.

By type of stain:

  • Coffee, tea and soda. Tannins. Blot without rubbing, lukewarm water and an upholstery cleaner with controlled pH. Never hot water straight on it: it fixes the tannin.
  • Grease, oil and sunscreen. Sunscreen is the Florida classic: an oily film that soaks into light leather and fabric. It needs a dedicated degreaser; the dish-soap trick usually leaves a ring.
  • Vomit and organic stains. Beyond the stain there are bacteria and odor, and heat makes both worse fast. Here steam and extraction are the only real solution.
  • Jeans dye on light leather. Very common and delicate. Rubbing with aggressive products damages the leather's finish; this is a job for a professional.
  • Pet hair and sand. Not stains, but both raise the price: they demand rubber tools and roughly double the vacuuming time before the cleaning can start.

What about store-bought products? They work on fresh surface stains. Their limit is physical: without an extraction machine, the dissolved dirt stays inside the foam. That is why many seats "cleaned" at home show a yellowish ring a few days later, when the moisture inside rises back to the surface; in Florida humidity it usually brings a smell with it.

What raises (or lowers) the price

Before asking for a quote, it helps to know what moves the final figure:

  • Vehicle size. A three-row SUV or a converted van means noticeably more surface and time than a compact.
  • Upholstery material. Leather needs different products and time. Alcantara, even more so.
  • Level of soiling. A smoker's interior, daily pets, beach sand ground into the carpet or old stains mean extra passes and extra time.
  • Headliner. Many cheap quotes leave it out. It is the most delicate surface in the cabin: excess moisture loosens it, and Florida heat and humidity already work against the adhesive holding it up.
  • Sanitizing extras. Ozone or certified disinfection add cost where they are not included; after a storm-soaked cabin, they are often worth it.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to clean a full car interior?

At SERRES the interior is priced by level: Refresh from {{PRICE:detailing.refresh}} for a vacuum and wipe-down, Deep Clean from {{PRICE:detailing.deep}} with steam and hot-water extraction, and Showroom Reset from {{PRICE:detailing.showroom}} for the full inside-and-out reset. The exact figure depends on the size of the vehicle and its condition, and we confirm it before we start.

How long does a professional upholstery cleaning take?

A Refresh takes around three hours. A Deep Clean with steam and extraction means booking the car in for the day, and part of that is drying time, which Florida humidity stretches. A Showroom Reset takes one to two days. Express services of under an hour only cover surface cleaning.

Does steam cleaning damage the seats?

No, if it is applied with the right equipment and technique. Controlled steam cleans and disinfects without soaking the foam, and it is gentler than methods that flood the fabric with water. The risk comes with domestic machines without moisture control, especially on the headliner.

Can every stain be removed from car seats?

Fresh stains almost always come out. Old ones set by heat and time fade in most cases, but some, such as dyes, chemical burns and bleach, leave a permanent mark. In the Florida sun a spill sets within days. A professional will tell you before starting what result is realistic.

How often should car upholstery be cleaned?

A deep cleaning once or twice a year is enough for normal use. With children, pets or smokers, every three to four months. A car that sees the beach regularly or sits outside through the wet season belongs at the more frequent end. Monthly basic maintenance extends the effect of the deep cleaning and costs less than recovering a degraded interior.
