The two do different jobs, and running both is the complete answer. Below, the data to decide with.

PPF vs ceramic coating: the difference in 30 seconds

  • PPF (paint protection film) is a transparent, self-healing polyurethane film, 7.5 mil thick — a physical barrier that absorbs rock chips, scuffs and scratches before they reach the paint.
  • A ceramic coating is a liquid SiO₂ treatment that cures on the clear coat into a layer 1 to 2 microns thick — a chemical barrier that repels water, dirt, tree sap, bird droppings, bug residue and UV.

Put another way: PPF protects the paint from hits, a ceramic coating protects it from the elements. In Boca Raton the elements are not an abstraction — near year-round UV, panels too hot to touch by noon, an afternoon storm most of the summer, sprinkler water on every driveway and salt air a couple of miles off the beach. The two products do not compete for the same job, so the question is not which is better. It is which one solves your problem.

What PPF does that a ceramic coating cannot

PPF is the only real protection against physical damage. At 7.5 mil the film is roughly a hundred times thicker than a cured coating, and that thickness buys concrete things:

  • Rock chips at highway speed. The stone hits the film and stops there. Without it, every mile of I-95 between Deerfield Beach and Fort Lauderdale is a lottery, and the truck lanes on US-1 throw grit all day long.
  • Sand and storm debris. Blown sand near the coast and the grit a summer storm drops on every road behave like a mild blast cabinet on a leading edge. Film takes the abrasion instead of the clear coat.
  • Parking-lot and trunk-lip scuffs. Shopping carts, bags, dog claws on the rear bumper. The film absorbs it.
  • Self-healing. Light wash marks close up with heat — and in Boca Raton the panel is already at healing temperature most afternoons, so the car does it by itself.
  • Reversibility. It comes off without harming the clear coat. On a lease return or a resale, the paint underneath is still factory.

The limit of PPF is financial, not technical: covering every painted panel costs more than any coating, which is why the most requested install is the front end — bumper, hood, fenders and mirrors — where the large majority of impacts land. The coverage ladder runs Partial Front $1,000, Full Front $1,900 (the most popular), Track Pack $2,600 and Full Body $4,995, each available in Essential (NAR H190) or Signature (3M Scotchgard Pro Series 200). What each one covers is on the paint protection film page.

What a ceramic coating does that PPF cannot

A ceramic coating changes how the paint behaves every single day. After the decon and gloss prep stage, and once the coating has cured:

  • Water does not sit on the car. Drops roll off and take the dirt with them. With an afternoon storm most of the summer and sprinklers hitting the driveway at dawn, that is the difference between hard-water spots etching into the clear coat and rinsing off with a hose.
  • Love bugs stop being an emergency. Two seasons a year, May and September, they arrive in sheets. Their residue is acidic and in Florida heat it can etch unprotected clear coat within days. On a coated panel it wipes off.
  • UV fade slows down. Near year-round sun is what chalks a red or black roof and hood on a car parked outside. A coating filters part of that and buys the paint years of looking new.
  • The gloss visibly goes up. The prep stage removes the haze first, so the finish gains real depth rather than a film of shine.
  • Washing takes a fraction of the time. Less dwell, less contact, less marring — which matters, because most swirl marks come from washing, not from the road.

The ceramic catalogue here is two lines, not a tier ladder:

  • 3-Year Ceramic Package, $1,200. Decon, single-stage gloss prep, coating and cure — the standard treatment for paint in good condition.
  • Top-up over PPF / wrap, $600. The same hydrophobics laid over fresh film or vinyl, without touching the paint underneath. This is the layer that fixes PPF's one weak point.

If the paint arrives swirled or oxidized, correction comes first and is quoted separately — Paint Correction (1–2 stage), $600, on the detailing page. Coating over defects locks them in.

What a ceramic coating will never do is stop a stone. The “9H hardness” the industry advertises is the pencil scale, not the mineral one: it resists wash marring, not gravel at 70 mph. Any shop that tells you otherwise is selling smoke. The full program is on the ceramic coating page.

Which is better, PPF or ceramic? It depends on these four scenarios

1. A new or nearly new car you plan to keep. Full Front at minimum — Essential $1,900, Signature $2,150. The paint is intact and the film freezes it in that state. If it sleeps outside, add the ceramic top-up over the film, $600, or take the New Car package ($2,990), which bundles full front PPF, ceramic and tint.

2. A daily driver on a contained budget. The 3-Year Ceramic Package, $1,200. You get the gloss back, cut the wash time and protect the clear coat from UV and chemical attack — the best result per dollar. If the front end is already taking hits, the Daily Driver package ($1,990) pairs partial front PPF with ceramic.

3. Lots of highway miles. Full Front, no hesitation, and Track Pack ($2,600) if the car spends real time at speed: it adds rocker panels, A-pillars and the leading roof edge, which is exactly where sand and gravel land once you are past 60. A respray never matches factory paint, and a chipped hood is visible from ten feet.

4. Lease or short-term resale. Full Front if the contract penalizes damage — the film comes off clean at hand-back. A ceramic coating if you only need the car to look right at return or in the listing photos.

Car paint protection: all the options, ranked

To put both products on the full map — PPF from $1,000, ceramic from $600 — here is the ladder from smallest investment to largest:

  1. Wax or sealant: a few months at best, less under Florida sun. A starting point, not protection.
  2. Ceramic coating — the 3-Year Ceramic Package, $1,200: chemical, UV and aesthetic protection for three years. No defense against impacts.
  3. PPF on the front end — Partial Front $1,000 or Full Front $1,900: physical protection exactly where the damage happens.
  4. PPF with the highway panels added — Track Pack $2,600: front end plus rockers, A-pillars and the leading roof edge.
  5. Full Body PPF — $4,995: every painted panel sealed against the road.
  6. PPF with a ceramic coating over it: the definitive combination — film for the hits, coating for the hydrophobics and the easy washing, on the film itself.

Option 6 is what collectors run, and it is why the top-up exists — $600 over fresh film: a coating bonds to PPF and fixes its only weak point, medium water behavior. The packages bundle the same idea — Daily Driver $1,990, New Car $2,990, Collector $5,990 — and every one of them is on the pricing page with what it includes. In this climate that second layer earns its keep: salt air, storm water and bug residue land on film exactly as they land on paint.

Frequently asked questions

Can you apply a ceramic coating on top of PPF?

Yes, and it is the most complete combination there is. The coating gives the film the hydrophobic behavior and the easy washing it does not have on its own, and it goes over fresh film without touching the paint underneath. That is the Top-up over PPF / wrap, $600. It is also bundled into our Daily Driver, New Car and Collector packages.

How long do PPF and a ceramic coating last in South Florida?

PPF carries the manufacturer film warranty — up to 10 years on the films we install — plus a 1-year SERRES installation warranty. Ceramic is sold as the 3-Year Ceramic Package. Boca Raton sun, heat and salt air make both work harder than they would inland, so the real figure depends on where the car sleeps and how it is washed: no brush car washes, no harsh chemicals.

Does a ceramic coating protect against rock chips and scratches?

Not against impacts. A cured coating is 1 to 2 microns thick and resists light wash marring, but a stone off I-95 or a key scrape goes straight through it to the paint. For physical damage the only effective protection is PPF.

Is it worth putting PPF only on the front end?

In most cases, yes. The hood, bumper, fenders and mirrors take the large majority of gravel impacts, which is why Full Front is the coverage most cars here get: Essential $1,900, Signature $2,150. You protect the critical zone and leave the rest of the car to a ceramic coating if looks and easy washing are the goal.

Does PPF damage the paint when it is removed?

No, as long as a professional installs and removes it. The film peels away leaving the original clear coat intact, with no adhesive residue. That is its advantage on a lease or at resale: underneath, the paint is as it was on day one.
